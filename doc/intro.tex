\chapter*{A General Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{A General Introduction}

This thesis follows a journey lasted almost five years and tries to summarize both the methodological knowledge acquired and the modelling skills practiced.

It started from a question: Which are the macroeconomic effects of a Universal Basic Income (UBI)?

From the beginning it was clear that a simple model would not be able to give a satisfying answer: a UBI is a redistributive policy, and so it needs a distribution to be applied on.
This idea puts the research inside the complexity framework which, particularly in physics, has developed the tools to link the micro with the macro, to manipulate distribution and heterogenous sets.
Chapter \ref{ch:complexity} explores the ambiguity of the word complexity and how it has been received differently in different economic research communities.
The chapter is written with a style close to the one of a scientific paper and in its conclusion sketch the institutional needs to see a particular kind of complexity approach to thrive in contemporary academia.
Chapter \ref{ch:against} further develops the ideas presented in the previous chapter (following the advancement in my research), moving from complexity to post-modernism and providing a more definitive and radical perspective, which is not entirely adopted in this thesis because of the institutional constraints about what is researchable in Economics.

The conclusion reached was to adopt an Agent-Based (AB) framework as a simulation technique suitable to link micro and macroeconomic theory.
From an economic point of view, the theoretical framework adopted are the Stock-Flow Consistent (SFC) models, which are better suited to model distributional effects because of their zero-sum behaviour.

From chapter \ref{ch:why} forward, the thesis moves towards the creation of a simple model that, including some even if not all the methodological conclusions of the previous chapters, tries to answer the initial question.
First, in chapters \ref{ch:why} and \ref{ch:choices} some modelling choices and economic hypothesis are justified.
Then chapters \ref{ch:implementation} and \ref{ch:chaos} focus on more technical aspects of the development.
Chapters \ref{ch:m0} to \ref{ch:m6} present the construction of the model step-by-step (as far as we know for the first time comparing aggregate and Agent-Based Stock-Flow Consistent models).
Finally, chapter \ref{ch:policy} shows how the model developed can be used to answer the initial question and other policy related ones.
