\chapter{Against this Thesis}
\label{ch:against}
In chapter \ref{ch:complexity} there is one highlighted sentence: "knowledge cannot but be understood as contextual and functional".
At the time of writing it I already understood that that was the core idea of the paper, but I was not able to argument it.

If I have to paraphrase it, it now will sound like "there is no true knowledge, but only knowledge produced by a group of subject with a specific goal, like predicting the future or giving coherence to their own experience".

But why no truth?
There is a centuries old debate about the possibility for a subject to know the ontological nature of the things (the noumenon) through their own perception (the phenomenon).
In my experience human perception is too heterogeneous to be universally reconciled into a single unifying point of view.
So, if truth is universal as in the positivist European tradition, there must be right and wrong perceptions.
Which arise the questions: "Which is the right criterion of truth for perceptions? Who is able to establish it? By which Authority?"

Shared and established knowledge is something that sounds with the perception of the people that accept it, that share some foundational beliefs (some axioms) which can justified by culture (and maybe by neurobiological traits).
This set of internalized beliefs is called in other social science the positioning.
More this set of internalized beliefs is shared and diffused in the society, stronger is its credibility and its authority.

Knowledge is contextual for this reason: if there is no a-priori and absolute criterion of truth, it has to emerge from a social consensus that is situated in space, time, and subjectivities.
It emerges from the relations among different individuals (subjects) when each one accepts to considered the perceptions of the others as good as their owns, thinking of itself as a part of a network rather than as a monad.
Knowledge then finds its place only inside intersubjectivity, in the relations among subjects that accept themselves as the owners of perceptions that can not be taken as truth.

This suggests the need to think about the reproduction mechanism of the internalized beliefs, but this idea will be addressed later.

If perception is too heterogeneous to be trusted in absolute, so there is no possibility to be sure to have chosen the right criterion to reach the ontological nature of things and the Truth.

What is the alternative?
How we can orientate the process of producing knowledge if truth is not a cardinal point?

To revert to the beginning: knowledge is functional.
In other words, we produce knowledge with a goal, and achieving that goal is the cardinal point.

An intuitive hypothesis for such a goal, having discarded the Truth, is to create a coherent representation (a model) of experience.
But experiences are so different that such a task is impossible, unless one focus only on themselves, without claiming any generality, or adopts madness as an epistemic practice, accepting each perception and experience as it, without aiming to find any connection or broader meaning.

In hard sciences it is quite simple to recognize (and agree to) the goal: predict the future.
Having a reliable expectation of the effect of doing is a need that humankind tries to address since the most ancient times, with different beliefs systems often referred to as religions.
The scientific method based on the consistency of repetitions is not able to discover the true functioning of the world, but to provide tools and knowledge to predict the consequences of an action, like building a bridge or mixing two substances.

In social sciences and humanities (including Economics) despite centuries of efforts future is still unpredictable.
Then the goal must be another one.

We think with the words that are given us, we read reality with the ideas we encountered in life. Unless we are able to create new knowledge (possibly disruptive and incoherent with the pre-existing one), existing knowledge shapes our perceptions, our understanding of the world, our choices, and life.

Shaping knowledge is shaping reality, being able to control the criterion of truth, the goal of knowledge is a form of power.
We can look at established knowledge and the process to create it asking "For whom is it useful? Which culture does it reproduce?".

So, knowledge is always moral and political.
In developing knowledge what to look for has to be chosen, how to look for it, which details are relevant and which can be approximated away, how things are signified.
This is the process of approximation described in the previous chapter.

What does it mean for Economics?
We are interpreting reality with our beliefs, we are building models trying to demonstrate that some effects that we consider desirable will appear, we are looking for the data that do no more that giving us the truth we already know.
We are mathematizing and hiding our positioning.

There are views of the economy and of Economics (in its assumptions and goals) that are simply contradictory, incompatible.
There is no dataset that can not be challenged, deformed, reinterpreted changing the (moral) assumptions from which we analyse it.

We have already sounded theories: I remember after COVID the post-Keynesian conference in Berlin in which all the keynote speakers were saying: "We have those models from the 80s that already describe effectively what we are seen today".
We already have detailed policies ready to be implemented. But we are not able to implement them. Why?

As research community (of left-wingy economists) we spend many of our words and energy to try to marginally improve our "scientific" description of reality, to better fit data.
Instead of working on our positioning, we should devote our energies in understanding first and narrating later what our right economy (and society) looks like.
We are trying to convince policy-maker with data rather than with ideology: but why those to whom power is given to reproduce inequalities should lower them?

This made me ask, a couple of years ago, why we should not focus more on the idea of hegemony and the tools for developing a counter-hegemonic imaginary.
Tools that can be built both as essay, mixing old ideas, and as tales, showing it in the daily life.
It would require us to rethink ourselves, from blue collar workers in the knowledge assembly line that the Academia has turned in, to being intellectuals and utopians that not only represent the world but also dream of it.

After this premise we can trace the steps back to introduction and try to dive into why the answer to "Would an UBI have inflationary effects?" is "It does not matter", or better "It is not relevant".

An UBI should be pursued because it would allow a different society, because it would free humans from exploitation. Even if it would cause inflation, it will be possible to manage it as we are already doing or, even better, by reducing profits margins (which leads to the question of how to get enough power to move the bargaining power from capital to labour, and then back to how to become hegemonic).

In other words, we can turn the table and rethink the practice of Economics: instead of using the technical knowledge we have to keep the growth steady and the prices constant, we can aim to a fairer society and use the very same knowledge (for example on how to manage inflation) to stabilize the economy as a necessity rather than a goal.

At a certain point of the journey, I strongly envisioned this thesis as an attempt to reconcile the collectivism of the Marxian tradition, with the need of being recognized as an autonomous subject expressed by post-modernism.
The hint I have is the intersubjectivity described above, as a way to prevent the intrasubjective approach to knowledge and relations ("I'm right, and the measure of everything") that is proposed by the neoliberal idea of human, a \textit{homo faber} or a \textit{homo oeconomicus}.
From there I think it is possible to explore the idea of a post-work society (or better a post-exploitation one) as a desirable future to let people fall in love with it.

But such a thesis is not possible, because disciplines in science have transformed themselfves from tools for organizing knowledge in a useful taxonomy, to barriers imposed to not think too much outside the existent, to not revolutionize the knowledge rather than reform it (and \textit{cui prodest}?).
As said, science itself is a moral matter immersed in the power dynamics of the society, and it is necessary to think of us politically as a part of it. Do we want to cooperate with the reaction, do we desert, do we find in the interstices the space to live inside the contradictions and attempt a slow revolution?

After four years the most urgent question I'm left with is: if not in Academia, where the future is dreamt?
