\chapter{Production of Capital by the means of Capital}
The next extension of the model is to make also the production of capital goods to rely on capital goods.
The reason behind it is to have each employed worked in the model matched with a capital good that can bring information about the salary or the skills required. This change aims to improve the matching mechanism down the line in the development of a model that differentiates workers and their wages.

The matrices of the model are very similar to the previous version with the only difference being the presence of a stock of capital goods for the capital firms sector.

\section{The aggregate version}
The same behavioural equation for the desired investment is used for both sectors, and the production of capital goods is divided between the two sector proportionally to the desired investments.

The resulting dynamics of the model is stable, at full employment and with unmatched demand for both goods.

When the government spending is set exogenous the behaviour is still cyclical but some lagged relations appear: for example the production of capital goods matches the desired investments except for the first few periods in which the demand increases, to allow the capital goods sector to increase its own stock of capital to accommodate the increment of the demand from the other sector.

\section{The matrix-based agent-based extension}
The only substantial difference in this version of the AB model is in the capital goods market.

First, each capital firm produces its own capital goods and does not enter the market as a buyer from other firms.

Consumption firms order all the capital goods to match their desired investments up to the potential output of capital firms (i.e. number of capital goods times their productivity). Then ordered investments and the desired production for self-investment are proportionally scaled down to match the realized production.

The dynamics is overall still very stable and at full employment, but the gap between the demand for consumption goods and the supply is wide. To stabilize the model, the mark-up rate has been increased compared to the aggregate version and the previous agent-based one, to compensate for the self-production of capital goods; otherwise the capital good sector would require a constant influx of monetary capital by the households. The demand of consumption firms for capital goods is no more matched and the rich-get-richer dynamics is amplified, since an economy short on the demand side forces a higher saving rate (figures \ref{fig:02AB_out} and \ref{fig:02AB_box}).
